# pet friendly hotels in orlando: what each hotel actually charges for your dog, and how to claw part of it back

Orlando has one of the deepest benches of pet-friendly hotels in the country — Trip.com's Orlando listing alone counts 143 properties that take pets [4]. The problem isn't finding one. It's that "pet friendly" covers everything from $15 a night to $150 a night, and the booking pages rarely show the real number until you're deep in the checkout flow.

So here's the part that actually decides your trip budget: the pet fee, whether it's charged per night or per stay, and the weight ceiling that disqualifies your dog at the front desk. Below is a comparison built from the hotels' own published policies and the visitor authority's rundown of Orlando pet fees, plus one thing most Orlando hotel guides skip entirely — the cashback available on the room charge itself, and why it does and doesn't offset the dog fee.

## The per-night versus per-stay distinction is the whole ballgame

Two hotels can both advertise "pet friendly, $150" and cost you wildly different amounts.

Rosen Inn, closest to Universal, charges **$15 per pet, per night** [1]. A five-night stay with one dog: $75.

Hyatt Regency Orlando charges **$150 per stay for up to six nights** [1]. Same five nights, same one dog: $150.

The gap doesn't come from the hotel being nicer. It comes from the billing structure. Per-night fees punish long stays; per-stay fees punish short ones. If you're doing three nights at a per-stay property, you're paying $50 a night for the dog. If you're doing eight nights at a per-night property charging $50, you're paying $400.

Run that math before you compare nightly rates. A hotel with a $20 higher room rate and a flat $50 pet fee can be cheaper than a $20-cheaper room with a $50-per-night pet charge.

## What the fee line hides

The dollar figure is usually the third most important thing on the policy page. These matter more with a dog in the car:

- **Weight ceilings.** Element Orlando at SeaWorld and several Hyatt properties cap at 50 to 75 pounds. Castle Hotel, Autograph Collection and Lake Nona Wave Hotel cap at 40. If your dog is a 90-pound Labrador, half this list is off the table.
- **Pet count.** Most Orlando properties allow two. Lake Nona Wave Hotel allows **one** dog per guestroom, and the fee jumps from $150 for a standard room to **$250 for a suite** [1].
- **Cats.** Hampton Inn Orlando International Airport, Drury Inn & Suites Near Universal, and Universal's Loews Sapphire Falls all explicitly take cats. Many others quietly don't [1][2][3].
- **Room-type restrictions.** Sapphire Falls only opens **Standard Two Queen rooms** to pets [3]. Book a suite and your dog isn't welcome, fee or no fee.
- **Unattended-pet rules.** Sapphire Falls does not allow pets to be left in the room, crated or otherwise [3]. That changes your theme park plan: someone stays behind, or you book a property with a different rule.
- **Advance notice.** Hyatt Regency Orlando asks you to flag your dog at least **three days before arrival** [1].

> Practical rule: call the property directly and get the pet policy in writing on your confirmation. Front desk enforcement varies, and a policy page updated two years ago is not a guarantee.

## Which one fits your actual trip

**If you're doing Universal for three or four days:** Rosen Inn at $15 per pet per night is the cheapest dog line item in this list by a wide margin, and it's the closest Rosen property to Universal. Loews Royal Pacific is the other end of that trade — you pay $100 and get walkable park access instead of a shuttle.

**If you're doing SeaWorld:** DoubleTree at SeaWorld is a $50 flat deposit and the property is officially partnered with the park, with early access and the QuickQueue program included [1].

**If the dog is large:** Drury Inn & Suites Near Universal (80 lbs combined for two) and Hampton Inn MCO (75 lbs per pet) are the two most generous weight allowances here. Anything at 40 lbs or below rules out most of the luxury tier.

**If you want the dog to be an actual guest rather than a problem:** The Alfond Inn runs a V.I.P. (Very Important Pup) package — two nights in a classic room with a specialty dog bowl, treats and toys, plus a donation to the Pet Alliance of Greater Orlando [1]. At $50 per night it's also mid-priced.

**If you're staying a week or more:** Hyatt Place's structure is the outlier — $75 covers the first six nights rather than the whole stay, and Hyatt House-style extended stays generally work better with a dog than a room with one chair and no floor space.

## FAQ

**Do Orlando hotels charge pet fees per night or per stay?**
Both, and it's inconsistent even within a brand. Rosen Inn charges $15 per pet per night. Hyatt Regency Orlando charges $150 per stay for up to six nights. Hyatt Place runs a middle structure: $75 covering the first six nights, with a $100 deep-cleaning fee added for stays of seven to 30 nights [1].

**Can I leave my dog in the room while I'm at the parks?**
Not at Sapphire Falls — pets can't be left unattended there, even crated [3]. Many other Orlando properties don't publish a clear answer, which is a question for the front desk when you call to confirm your reservation.

**Are cats allowed?**
Some are. Hampton Inn Orlando International Airport accepts dogs and cats [2], as do Drury Inn & Suites Near Universal [1] and Loews Sapphire Falls [3]. Plenty of "pet friendly" Orlando hotels mean dogs only.
